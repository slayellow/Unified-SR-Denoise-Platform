# Knowledge distillation in efficient / mobile image restoration challenges and industry practice

Scope: how winning and top teams in NTIRE / MAI / AIM efficient-restoration challenges (2021-2026) and industry papers use teachers and KD, what gains they attribute to KD, and how KD sits with reparameterization and quantization. Use case being evaluated (from the brief): a ~41k-param, 32-ch, half-resolution-body Bicubic++-style same-resolution denoiser (stride-2 stem, 4 ECB-reparameterized 3x3 convs, PixelShuffle(2), global input residual), deployed W16A16 on QCS8550 HTP at ~8 ms for 1280x720.

Method note: numbers below were read from the papers' own text (arXiv / CVF PDFs, extracted with pdftotext), unless marked otherwise. "Inferences" are mine, not the authors'.

## Q1. NTIRE Efficient SR challenges 2022-2025 (DIPNet, SPAN, RLFN, and others): who used KD, how, and what KD ablation gains are reported?

### Takeaway
KD went from absent (NTIRE 2022) to common among top-10 teams (2024) to the overall winner's method (2025, EMSR/DSCLoRA). The KD ablations that exist report small PSNR gains on bicubic x4 SR: about +0.05 dB (DIPNet, feature + output KD), +0.06 dB from teacher-enhanced GT on the student (DIPNet), and about +0.01 to +0.04 dB (DSCLoRA, on top of an already-trained SPAN-Tiny). Most team descriptions give no KD ablation at all.

### Cited Findings
**DIPNet (Megvii et al., CVPRW 2023, NTIRE 2023 ESR entry)**
- Pipeline: train a large HAT (Hybrid Attention Transformer) teacher; distill into an RLFN-derived student built from reparameterizable RRFB blocks; reparameterize back to RLFB after convergence; then iterative L2 filter pruning. — [DIPNet, arXiv 2304.07018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018)
- "Model-guided ground-truth enhancement": they "first train a large network with Hybrid Attention Transformer backbone for 1x super-resolution", run the original HR through it to get an enhanced HR `I_enh`, and train x4 SR on (LR, `I_enh`). Unlike HGGT they do no manual patch selection and keep flat patches. — [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018)
- Enhanced-GT ablation (Table 3, DIV2K val PSNR): teacher 31.27 dB with original GT vs 31.20 dB with enhanced GT (enhanced GT hurts the teacher, -0.07). Student 28.94 dB with original GT vs 29.00 dB with enhanced GT (+0.06). The authors explain that the big network "already has the ability to learn most kinds of details, and the use of enhanced ground-truth makes it learn more noise", while the small student benefits. — [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018)
- Feature-KD ablation (Table 4, DIV2K val PSNR): no KD 28.95; shallow features (first three blocks) 28.96; deep feature (last block) 28.98; both 29.00 dB. The authors summarise this as "about 0.05dB" over direct end-to-end training. — [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018)
- Losses: `L_feat = sum_i lambda_i ||F_i^T - psi(F_i^S)||_1` over 4 anchor depths (psi is a channel-expansion layer), plus `L_out = ||T(LR) - S(LR)||_1 + ||S(LR) - I_enh||_1`, with `L_dis = L_feat + L_out`. Then progressive fine-tuning with L2 against `I_enh`. — [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018)
- Schedule: teacher training and distillation use lr 1e-4 halved every 100k iterations with L1. Progressive fine-tuning uses lr 2e-5 halved every 20k, with patch size growing over [64, 128, 256, 384]. Pruning is 3 rounds at 5% each, with 384x384 fine-tuning after each round. — [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018)
- Pruning cost (Table 6): 29.042 → 29.034 → 29.018 → 29.001 dB over 3 rounds. Iterative L2 pruning (29.001) beats one-shot L2 (28.894). Final entry: 0.243M params, 14.90 GFLOPs, 18.30 ms, 29.04 dB, which the authors claim was the fastest runtime among NTIRE 2023 ESR participants. — [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018)

**NTIRE 2022 ESR (RLFN won)**
- The NTIRE 2022 ESR report mentions KD only in its introduction, as a general compression family. No team method section in the extracted text uses "teacher" or "knowledge distillation". — [NTIRE 2022 ESR report, arXiv 2205.05675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.05675)
- The RLFN paper (the 2022 winning design and the NTIRE 2024 baseline) contains no teacher or KD in its text. — [RLFN, arXiv 2205.07514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07514)

**NTIRE 2024 ESR (34 valid teams)**
- The organizers' introduction says "Among these, knowledge distillation stands out as particularly promising". Their "Main Ideas" and "Conclusions" stress reparameterization ("All the top three teams (i.e., XiaomiMM, Cao Group, and BSR) used this operation"), pruning, LSDIR pre-training, and multi-phase training "with increased patch size and reduced learning rate". — [NTIRE 2024 ESR report, arXiv 2404.10343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343)
- XiaomiMM (1st) submitted SPAN. The SPAN paper states that for the NTIRE 2024 entry (6 SPABs, 28 channels) "NGswin is used as the teacher model for boosting the restoration performance". Schedule: lr 5e-4 halved every 2e5 iterations, 1e6 iterations with L1, the setting repeated 4 times from the loaded weights, then L1+L2 fine-tuning at lr 1e-5 for 5e5 iterations at 512 HR patches. No ablation of the teacher's effect is given. — [SPAN, arXiv 2311.12770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12770)
- CMVG (5th in the report's results-table order): RDEN uses a HAT teacher trained from scratch, then a fixed teacher with a 2x-pretrained student initialisation. Loss is `sum_i lambda_i ||F_T^i - F_S^i|| + mu ||Y_T - X_S||`, with features from teacher blocks 0/2/4/5 and 1x1 convs to expand student channels. KD is combined with reparameterization. — [NTIRE 2024 ESR report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343)
- ECNU MViC (8th): IFADNet, 36 channels, teacher = large pre-trained SAFMN. Trained with L1 plus L1 distillation. — [NTIRE 2024 ESR report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343)
- HiSR (9th): SlimRLFN with reparameterizable convs, "light distillation" from a large-RLFN teacher, and iterative pruning. The teacher was trained for 100 epochs at lr 1e-3. The student used Charbonnier against GT plus Charbonnier against teacher output, "then they repeat this stage two more times without distillation loss". — [NTIRE 2024 ESR report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343)
- MViC SR (10th): SAFMN-large teacher; L1 to GT plus distillation to teacher prediction for 1000k iterations. BU-ESR (19th): HAT teacher and RFDN-based student with feature supervision. BlingBling (22nd): DVMSR (Vision Mamba) with a frozen 180-channel teacher and L1 feature alignment. — [NTIRE 2024 ESR report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343)
- None of the 2024 team sections I read give a with/without-KD PSNR number. — [NTIRE 2024 ESR report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343)

**NTIRE 2025 ESR (43 valid teams)**
- EMSR (1st overall, 0.131M params) uses a SPAN-Tiny-based student (26 ch) with ConvLoRA, merged at inference. Distillation is feature spatial-affinity KD after each block plus output-level L1 to the teacher, alongside the GT reconstruction loss. Stage 1 is 192 patches, batch 8, lr 1e-4, 30k iterations; stage 2 is 256 patches; EMA throughout. — [NTIRE 2025 ESR report, arXiv 2504.10686](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10686)
- EMSR's own paper (DSCLoRA, SJTU + Transsion): the teacher is pre-trained SPAN. Loss is `1*L_rec + 0.1*L_TS (pixel KD, L1) + 0.01*L_AD (spatial affinity KD, L1)`. Only the LoRA parameters are trained, on frozen pre-trained SPAN-Tiny weights (fine-tuning, not from scratch). Training is 30k iterations at 192 patches, then 30k at 256, lr 1e-4. Finally the merged weights are averaged with the original weights. — [DSCLoRA, arXiv 2504.11271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11271)
- DSCLoRA gains (Table 3, x4 PSNR): SPAN-Tiny → DSCLoRA is Set5 31.7252 → 31.7332 (+0.008), Urban100 25.5544 → 25.5774 (+0.023), Manga109 29.5629 → 29.5998 (+0.037). Table 5 compares no-distillation vs SPAN-ch52 vs SPAN-ch28 teachers. The best column equals the final model (Urban100 25.5774). The other columns sit around Urban100 25.5566-25.5567 and Manga109 29.567-29.568, so the KD part is about +0.02-0.03 dB. Column labels were scrambled in the extracted text, so which teacher won is unverified. — [DSCLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11271)
- mbga (7th): ESPAN with reparam blocks plus RIFE-style self-distillation from a co-trained teacher (student + 3 extra SPABs, online, not frozen). The extra blocks are then progressively removed over multi-step 500k-iteration stages with MSE and frequency losses. No KD gain is given. — [NTIRE 2025 ESR report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10686)
- mmSR (11th) added images generated by networks such as OmniSR to the training data (a form of teacher-generated data). BVIVSR uses multi-teacher KD (SRFormer, MambaIR, EDSR aggregated via MTKD) into a 154.8K student. Davinci (5th) explored KD but did not submit it. — [NTIRE 2025 ESR report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10686)
- Organizer conclusion: distillation "significantly contributed" to gains with no added inference complexity. No numbers back this. Reparameterization is "crucial" and common among top teams. Training is multi-phase with growing patch and decaying lr, and LSDIR pre-training helps significantly. — [NTIRE 2025 ESR report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10686)

### Inferences
- In bicubic-x4 ESR, the KD effect is on the order of the challenges' PSNR threshold margins: +0.01 to +0.06 dB. Teams use it because those few hundredths decide whether a smaller or faster model clears the fixed PSNR floor (26.90 / 26.99 dB in 2024). It is not a large quality lever.
- In this setting, teacher-enhanced GT (DIPNet) gave a gain the same size as feature KD (+0.06 vs +0.05 dB), and the two stack (final 29.00-29.04). This is the variant most relevant to a denoiser, where "enhanced GT" equals cleaner targets.
- No paper I read shows KD hurting a reparameterized student. The common order is: train with the expanded training graph plus KD, fold, then fine-tune or prune.

### Gaps
- The NTIRE 2023 ESR report itself (Li et al., CVPRW 2023) could not be downloaded (timeout). DIPNet's official rank and other 2023 KD teams are therefore not verified from the report.
- NTIRE 2024 ranks above come from the order of team names in the results table, whose extracted text was column-scrambled. They are approximate.
- No 2024/2025 team section reports a with/without-KD ablation except via the separate DSCLoRA paper.

## Q2. Mobile AI (MAI) and AIM challenges (quantized SR / denoising on mobile NPUs): do top solutions use KD, and with what gains? Any denoising-specific lightweight KD?

### Takeaway
In the MAI quantized-SR challenges of 2021 and 2022 (INT8 on NPUs), KD was rare: one top-2 team in 2022, and none found in 2021. QAT plus NPU-friendly operator choices were the decisive tricks. KD becomes explicit in MAI 2026, where the denoising entry with KD reports a large gain, but that gain is confounded and comes from a 1.96M student on a 650-pair real-noise dataset. An MAI 2026 INT8 SR entry reports only +0.09 dB from teacher guidance.

### Cited Findings
- MAI 2021 Real-Time Quantized SR (INT8, mobile NPUs): no team description in the extracted text mentions teacher or distillation. Organizers note that most teams "were not able to fight the accuracy drop resulted from model quantization". One team dropped separable convs because of the large accuracy drop after quantization. — [MAI 2021 report, arXiv 2105.07825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07825)
- MAI & AIM 2022 Quantized SR (INT8, Synaptics VS680, x3): winner Z6 (SCSRN, >50 FPS) used "weights clipping and normalization". Second place TCLResearchEurope (>62 FPS, 1080p@60) "used a combination of the convolution re-parametrization, channel pruning and model distillation techniques". The best fidelity, TCL Research HK, came from depth, reparameterization and many skip connections. No KD ablation number is quoted. — [MAI 2022 report, arXiv 2211.05910](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05910)
- MAI 2022 organizer recipe: a shallow net; ≤3x3 convs; depth-to-space for upsampling; reparameterized conv blocks fused at inference; 1x1 conv instead of nearest upsampling; clip outputs before depth-to-space; QAT fine-tuning before INT8 conversion; transfer learning. They also state that "the accuracy drop caused by FP32 → INT8 conversion is less than 0.1 dB in the majority of cases". KD is not in the recipe list. — [MAI 2022 report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05910)
- MAI 2026 Image Denoising Challenge (650 noisy/clean training pairs, 50 val; evaluated at 2432x3200; runtime at FP16 1088x1920; official ranking by GPU runtime), entry from Kayani et al. (Univ. Würzburg, CVPRW 2026): teacher is a 3-level U-Net (64-512 ch), 41.6M params, 37.71 dB, frozen. The student is NPU-native (3x3 conv, ReLU, nearest-neighbour upsampling + 3x3 instead of transposed conv), 1.96M params. — [Kayani et al., arXiv 2605.03680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03680), [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026W/MAI/html/Kayani_Real_Image_Denoising_with_Knowledge_Distillation_for_High-Performance_Mobile_NPUs_CVPRW_2026_paper.html)
- Loss: `L = 100*||S-GT||^2 + 900*||S-T||^2 + 50*L1` ("high-alpha", alpha = 0.9). The authors' rationale: weighting the teacher heavily prevents "the student from overfitting to residual sensor noise present in the raw ground truth data". Training: Adam, cosine (Tmax 200, eta_min 1e-5), grad-clip 0.1, 200 epochs plus 20-30 fine-tune epochs, flips and 90° rotations. "Progressive context expansion" trains on 256 crops, then fine-tunes at 512 and 1024 crops. — [Kayani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03680)
- Ablation (Table 1, full-res val PSNR): 12-filter 0.35M student 34.86 dB (26.4 ms); 16-filter 1.96M student without KD 36.08 dB; same student with KD 37.66 dB (46.1 ms on Snapdragon 8 Elite NPU, 34.0 ms on Dimensity 9500). That is +1.58 dB, with the teacher gap shrinking from 1.63 to 0.05 dB. Paired per-image analysis: teacher 37.71 vs student 37.63 dB, mean gap 0.08 dB (95% CI [-0.00, 0.16]). The "distilled" stage is also the stage that adds progressive context expansion, so the +1.58 dB is not KD alone. The 0.35M student was not distilled. Deployment was FP16 TFLite, not INT8. — [Kayani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03680)
- MAI 2026 Quantized 4K SR (INT8, x3), Nguyen et al.: a 3-stage recipe of spatial supervision, then Charbonnier + DCT + "confidence-weighted output-level distillation" from a Mamba teacher, then QAT on the fused (reparameterized) deploy graph with weight clipping and BN recalibration. Ablation: dynamic INT8 TFLite 29.91 dB without teacher vs 30.0003 dB with teacher guidance (+0.09 dB); fixed-shape INT8 30.006 dB; test 29.79 dB. — [Nguyen et al., arXiv 2604.20291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20291)
- NTIRE 2026 Mobile Real-World SR (perceptual): organizers say "Knowledge distillation and architectural compression are mandatory for mobile deployment", citing adversarial distillation, pseudo-ground-truth generation and pruning. Example: SamsungAICamera distilled one-step diffusion OSEDiff into a pruned U-Net + TAESD decoder. This is a generative/perceptual track, unlike a fidelity denoiser. — [NTIRE 2026 Mobile Real-World SR report, arXiv 2604.17306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17306)
- AIM 2025 Real-World RAW Image Denoising and Low-Light RAW Video Denoising challenges: the search snippets focus on noise synthesis and data. No KD-based lightweight track was found. — [AIM 2025 RAW denoising, arXiv 2510.06601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.06601), [AIM 2025 low-light RAW video denoising, arXiv 2508.16830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16830)

### Inferences
- The one large denoising KD gain (+1.58 dB) has three features absent from the use case: (a) real-noise GT with residual noise and only 650 pairs, where the teacher acts as a denoised-GT generator and regulariser; (b) a 1.96M student, about 48x larger than 41k; (c) a confounded ablation that adds 512/1024-crop fine-tuning in the same step. Expect much less when GT is clean, data is plentiful or synthetic, and the student is tiny.
- In fidelity SR under INT8 (MAI), the field converged on reparameterization + QAT + clipping rather than KD. Where KD appears (TCL 2022, Nguyen 2026), its measured effect is ≤0.1 dB.

### Gaps
- The official MAI 2026 denoising challenge report (team ranking and other teams' methods) was not retrieved. Kayani et al. state that their submission had the highest held-out test PSNR among listed methods, but the ranking is GPU-runtime-based and their rank is not stated.
- The details of TCLResearchEurope's distillation in MAI 2022 (teacher, loss, gain) were not found in the report text I extracted.
- I found no MAI or AIM "efficient denoising" challenge before 2026 with a lightweight-plus-KD track.

## Q3. Bicubic++ (CVPRW 2023, NTIRE 2023 RTSR) and similar ultra-light networks: did the authors or later teams use KD?

### Takeaway
Bicubic++ itself uses no KD; its gains come from multi-stage pruning and bias removal. In the same NTIRE 2023 Real-Time 4K SR challenge, Team ALONG's RUNet, the closest architectural analogue to the user's student (pixel-unshuffle + RepBlocks + pixel-shuffle, 32 ch × 3 blocks for x2), did use output KD from a HAT-L teacher. No KD gain was reported.

### Cited Findings
- Bicubic++ (Aselsan, CVPRW 2023) "won NTIRE 2023 RTSR Track 2 ×3 SR competition and is the fastest among all competitive methods". It adds about 1 dB over bicubic and runs about 1.17 ms on an RTX3090 for 720p→4K in FP16. The method is reversible downscaled features, end-to-end global structured pruning optimised for validation PSNR, and bias removal. All stages are trained for 1000 epochs on JPEG-Q90 DIV2K patches (108x108 LR). The paper text contains no teacher or KD. — [Bicubic++, arXiv 2305.02126](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.02126)
- NTIRE 2023 RTSR report, "Main Ideas" of the most competitive solutions: (1) reparameterization, (2) pixel shuffle/unshuffle, (3) multi-stage training, "since the neural networks are extremely constrained and shallow". KD is not in the main-ideas list. — [NTIRE 2023 RTSR report (Conde et al., CVPRW 2023)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/NTIRE/papers/Conde_Efficient_Deep_Models_for_Real-Time_4K_Image_Super-Resolution._NTIRE_2023_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf)
- Team ALONG (RUNet, section 3.3 of the report) uses pixel-unshuffle first so that "the majority of calculations are performed within a smaller resolution space", then RepBlocks, pixel-shuffle and a skip connection. They "also use Knowledge Distillation" with teacher supervision from HAT-L outputs plus data supervision from GT. L1 is used for reconstruction and L2 during fine-tuning and KD. The x2 student has 32 channels and 3 RepBlocks with unshuffle/shuffle scale 3; x3 has 64 channels and 5 blocks. — [NTIRE 2023 RTSR report](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/NTIRE/papers/Conde_Efficient_Deep_Models_for_Real-Time_4K_Image_Super-Resolution._NTIRE_2023_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- RUNet shows that output-level KD from a very large teacher (HAT-L, tens of M params) into a 32-channel, 3-block, unshuffled reparam student is practical. Without an ablation, though, it gives no evidence of size. The report's own list of decisive ideas omits KD.
- Bicubic++'s authors got their quality-latency trade-off without KD, which suggests KD was not needed to win at this extreme-light scale. This is weak evidence: absence of use is not evidence of no effect.

### Gaps
- No later paper was found that distills a teacher specifically into Bicubic++ or reports a KD ablation at the tens-of-k-param scale.
- RUNet's final rank could not be read reliably from the extracted Table 1, whose columns were scrambled.

## Q4. Industry / academic reports on KD for on-device ISP / denoising, including quantization-aware KD

### Takeaway
Industry-authored papers show KD helping small denoisers mainly when data is limited (Meta-affiliated FAN-KD: +0.63 dB RGB PSNR from simple output KD). Qualcomm's own mobile-SR work gets INT8 within 0.04-0.10 dB of FP16 through QAT and architecture tricks, with no KD. Quantization-aware KD papers (2026) exist but report modest or relative gains. For W16A16, quantization loss is not the bottleneck KD would fix.

### Cited Findings
- Feature-Align Network with KD for efficient RAW denoising (Young et al., arXiv 2021; targets "smartphones and smartwatches"). The loss ablation (Table 3, RAW/RGB PSNR): Charbonnier 45.668 / 34.869; simple output KD `|S - T|` 45.813 / 35.499 (+0.145 RAW, +0.630 RGB); feature-matching KD (teacher as perceptual net) 45.483 / 34.607, but best LPIPS (0.246 vs 0.289). The authors say KD is effective "especially in settings with limited training datasets". Their model is about 89.7 KB of parameters, 2.67 GMACs/MP, DND RAW 48.28 dB vs CycleISP 49.13 dB at 263x fewer MACs. — [FAN-KD, arXiv 2103.01524](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01524) (Facebook affiliation per search snippet; not verified in the extracted text)
- QuickSRNet (Qualcomm AI Research, CVPRW 2023), AIMET W8A8 (Table 4, x2 BSD100). Plain PTQ drops 0.70-1.08 dB from FP16; QAT drops 0.10-0.27; per-channel QAT 0.04-0.10; per-channel AdaRound 0.05-0.08. Identity initialisation plus ReLU1 clipping improve quantization robustness, and an input-to-output connection makes the architecture "significantly more robust to quantization". AIMET-simulated accuracy matched on-target within about 0.02 dB. The paper text has no KD or teacher. — [QuickSRNet, arXiv 2303.04336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04336)
- Quantization-aware distilled restoration (QDR, arXiv 2026, Sharif et al.) names three problems when combining QAT and KD in low-level vision: teacher-student capacity mismatch, spatial error amplification in decoder distillation, and a reconstruction-vs-distillation loss "tug-of-war" under quantization noise. Fixes are FP32 self-distillation, bottleneck-only "decoder-free" distillation and learnable loss reweighting. INT8 recovers 96.5% of FP32 performance (abstract only; per-task PSNR not retrieved). — [QDR, arXiv 2603.09624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09624)
- MAI 2026 INT8 SR (Nguyen et al.) put teacher distillation before QAT on the fused graph. The teacher gave +0.09 dB at INT8 (see Q2). — [arXiv 2604.20291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20291)
- DSCLoRA (EMSR, NTIRE 2025 winner) is co-authored with Transsion (handset maker), and DIPNet is from Megvii. Both use KD in GPU-measured challenge entries, not NPU deployments. — [DSCLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11271), [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018)
- Samsung (SamsungAICamera) used diffusion-teacher distillation in a perceptual mobile SR track (2026). — [NTIRE 2026 Mobile Real-World SR report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17306)

### Inferences
- The use case is W16A16. The INT8 literature shows that QAT plus clipping plus a global residual already brings INT8 within about 0.1 dB of float. At 16-bit, quantization error should be smaller still, so "KD to recover quantization loss" has little to recover. This is consistent with the project's memory note that W16 QAT training is vestigial.
- If KD is tried, the QDR warning about loss "tug-of-war" argues for doing KD in float first and running QAT/W16 conversion afterwards, which is the Nguyen et al. order.
- FAN-KD's gain was in RGB after an ISP (+0.63 dB) and much smaller in RAW (+0.145 dB). Where the loss is measured matters. For a same-resolution denoiser, expect the RAW-like (smaller) number unless the teacher removes noise that the GT still contains.

### Gaps
- No Google, Apple or Huawei paper on KD for an on-device denoiser with an ablation was found in this pass. Huawei's FAKD (feature-affinity KD for SR, ICIP 2020) is cited by DSCLoRA as the source of the affinity loss, but its own numbers were not retrieved.
- QDR's per-task PSNR numbers and which IR tasks it covers were not retrieved (abstract only).
- LiteKD-Net (arXiv 2608.05739, mobile denoising with feature KD) surfaced only as a search snippet; its numbers were not read.

## Q5. Recurrent practical recipe details: loss weighting, scratch vs fine-tune, frozen vs online teacher, duration, augmentation

### Takeaway
The dominant recipe is: train (or reuse) a much larger frozen teacher → train the student with GT loss plus output-level KD (often plus feature KD through 1x1 adapters) using L1 → fine-tune with L2/MSE at progressively larger patches and lower lr → fold the reparameterization (and optionally prune) → QAT if quantized. Weightings range from GT-dominant (DSCLoRA: KD 0.1 and 0.01) through equal (DIPNet) to teacher-dominant (Kayani: 9:1 teacher:GT), and the teacher-dominant case is justified only by noisy GT.

### Cited Findings
- Teacher type and size: HAT / HAT-L (DIPNet, CMVG, BU-ESR, RUNet); SAFMN-large (ECNU MViC, MViC SR); large RLFN, i.e. a "light but efficient teacher" (HiSR); NGswin (SPAN 2024); pre-trained SPAN (EMSR); multi-teacher SRFormer + MambaIR + EDSR (BVIVSR); 41.6M U-Net (MAI 2026 denoising); Mamba teacher (MAI 2026 INT8 SR). — [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018), [NTIRE 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343), [SPAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12770), [NTIRE 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10686), [NTIRE 2023 RTSR](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/NTIRE/papers/Conde_Efficient_Deep_Models_for_Real-Time_4K_Image_Super-Resolution._NTIRE_2023_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf), [Kayani](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03680), [Nguyen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20291)
- Loss weighting examples:
  - DIPNet: `L1(S,T) + L1(S,I_enh) + sum lambda_i L1(features)`, with lambda_i values not stated. — [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018)
  - DSCLoRA: rec 1, pixel-KD 0.1, affinity-KD 0.01. — [DSCLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11271)
  - Kayani: MSE-to-GT 100, MSE-to-teacher 900, L1 50. — [Kayani](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03680)
  - HiSR: Charbonnier GT + Charbonnier teacher, then KD dropped in later stages. — [NTIRE 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343)
  - Nguyen: "confidence-weighted" output KD. — [Nguyen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20291)
- Scratch vs fine-tune: DSCLoRA fine-tunes only LoRA adapters on a frozen pre-trained student (30k+30k iterations). CMVG initialises the student from a pre-trained 2x model. DIPNet and Kayani distill, then fine-tune. HiSR distills first and fine-tunes without KD. — [DSCLoRA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11271), [NTIRE 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343), [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018), [Kayani](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03680)
- Frozen vs online: frozen teachers in nearly all cases (Kayani "fixed high-capacity supervisor"; CMVG "teacher network is fixed"; BlingBling "remains fixed"). The exception is mbga (NTIRE 2025), with a co-trained, larger self-distillation teacher whose extra blocks are progressively removed. — [Kayani](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03680), [NTIRE 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343), [NTIRE 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10686)
- Duration and patches: DIPNet patches 64→384, lr 2e-5 fine-tune. SPAN 1M iterations ×4 repeats, then 500k at 512 patches. mbga 500k-iteration steps up to 512 patches. Kayani 200 + 20-30 epochs, crops 256→512→1024. Organizers in 2024 and 2025 describe multi-phase training with growing patch / shrinking lr as near-universal. — [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018), [SPAN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.12770), [NTIRE 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10686), [Kayani](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03680), [NTIRE 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343)
- Augmentation: flips and 90° rotations are standard (Kayani; ECNU MViC; MAI 2022 teams). No KD-specific augmentation (e.g. mixup for KD, or teacher on extra unlabelled data) was reported, except mmSR adding network-generated images and Davinci's mixup without KD. — [Kayani](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03680), [NTIRE 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343), [MAI 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05910), [NTIRE 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10686)
- Interaction with reparameterization: KD is applied to the training-time (expanded) graph and the student is folded afterwards (DIPNet RRFB → RLFB; CMVG RDEN; RUNet RepBlocks; mbga; Nguyen QAT on the fused graph). Feature KD needs channel-expansion adapters (DIPNet channel expand; CMVG 1x1 conv), and BlingBling skipped feature KD when channels mismatched. — [DIPNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07018), [NTIRE 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10343), [NTIRE 2023 RTSR](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023W/NTIRE/papers/Conde_Efficient_Deep_Models_for_Real-Time_4K_Image_Super-Resolution._NTIRE_2023_CVPRW_2023_paper.pdf), [Nguyen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20291)

### Inferences
- For a 41k, 32-ch, 4-conv half-resolution student, output-level KD (or teacher-cleaned targets, DIPNet-style) is the only cheap variant. Feature KD across a deep teacher and a 4-layer student would need adapters and arbitrary layer matching, and DIPNet's feature KD was worth only +0.05 dB at 243k params.
- The bicubic-SR evidence suggests an expected gain of +0.0x dB. The denoising evidence suggests up to about +0.6-1.6 dB, but only with noisy or limited GT and much larger students. Which regime applies depends on how clean the denoiser's GT is (synthetic clean GT → SR-like small gains; real paired GT with residual noise → teacher-target gains possible). This is my synthesis, not a reported result.
- A cheap, falsifiable first test consistent with the field: generate teacher outputs once (offline pseudo-GT, as in DIPNet/mmSR), train the existing student on a mix of GT and teacher targets, and compare on the existing evaluation protocol. This needs no change to the deployed graph, folding or W16A16 export.
- Capacity gap: no source tested a teacher-to-student ratio near 1000x at the tens-of-k scale. DSCLoRA's teachers (SPAN ch52 / ch28) were near the student's size, and Kayani's undistilled 0.35M model was not distilled. Whether a much larger teacher helps a 41k student more than a moderately larger one is untested in these sources (DSCLoRA's Table 5 column labels could not be verified).

### Gaps
- No source reports how long the KD stage needs relative to plain training, or a sweep of GT:teacher weight, except Kayani's single chosen alpha = 0.9 (and the Optuna search mention).
- No source reports KD combined with W16A16 specifically; all quantized results are INT8 (or FP16 deployment).
